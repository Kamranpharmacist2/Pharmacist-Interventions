# Pharmacist-Interventions
Clinical Outcomes of Pharmacist Interventions in Critical Care
This study evaluated the clinical and patient outcomes associated with pharmacist interventions in a critical care setting at a private hospital in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia.

A 6-month cross-sectional study was conducted from January to June 2016 involving adult patients in a 30-bed critical care unit. Clinical pharmacists monitored patients during daily medical rounds and documented medication-related interventions using an international pharmacist intervention model.

A total of 1,222 pharmacist interventions were provided for 1,124 adult patients. The most frequently documented intervention severity was potentially significant (49.9%), followed by potentially serious interventions (29.5%). Anti-infective medications represented the largest medication category involved in interventions (25.9%), followed by cardiovascular (13.5%) and gastrointestinal medications (10.2%).

Among documented patient outcomes, 33.4% were associated with improvement in the patient's condition, while 10.9% reached a therapeutic endpoint.

The study demonstrates the role of clinical pharmacists in identifying and addressing medication-related problems and supporting safe and effective medication use in critical care.
